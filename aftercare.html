Aftercare the aftermath

00

The first hour

before you get home

You leave wrapped. Check which wrap you have before you touch anything.

Cling film or a paper pad: take it off after two to four hours. Do not put it back on.

Adhesive film (the clear second-skin sheet): leave it on three to five days. Fluid and ink will pool under it, which is normal. If it lifts or fills up, take it off early, wash, and follow the day 1–3 routine.

Do

  • Wash your hands before the wrap comes off. Every single time.
  • Take the film off in a warm shower — it peels away instead of pulling.
  • Peel it back along the skin, not straight up.

Don't

  • Re-wrap it in cling film overnight. It needs air.
  • Let anyone touch it, however clean they say their hands are.
  • Panic at the colour of the fluid under the film.
01

Wash and thin

day one to three

A new tattoo is an open wound. Keep it clean and dry. Wash it twice a day, three times if you have been sweating.

  1. Clean hands, lukewarm water — not hot.
  2. Fragrance-free liquid soap, worked gently with fingertips only.
  3. Rinse until it stops feeling slippery.
  4. Pat dry with kitchen paper, never a bath towel.
  5. Air-dry ten minutes, then the thinnest possible layer of ointment.

Use a thin layer. If it looks shiny, it is too much. Blot the rest off. Too much ointment slows healing and can lift ink.

Do

  • Use fragrance-free soap and unscented ointment or lotion.
  • Wear loose cotton over it. Sleep on clean sheets.
  • Expect soreness, warmth and a little redness at the edges.

Don't

  • No soaking. No baths, pools, lakes, sea or hot tubs.
  • No scrubbing, no flannels, no exfoliant, no antibacterial gel.
  • No sunbeds, no direct sun, no gym mats, no pets on the bed.
02

The peel and the itch

day four to fourteen

From about day four it flakes, and the flakes come off the colour of the ink. That is skin, not your tattoo. Around day six it itches.

Swap the ointment for plain unscented lotion once the surface is dry rather than raw. Keep it lightly moisturised, not greasy.

Do not scratch. Scratching pulls off scabs, and scabs take the ink with them. If the itch is bad, press a cool clean cloth over it.

Do

  • Let flakes fall off on their own schedule.
  • Keep washing twice daily right through the peel.
  • Expect it to look dull and cloudy near the end. That is normal.

Don't

  • Never pick, peel or scratch. This is the only rule that matters.
  • Don't shave over it until every flake is gone.
  • Don't judge the piece yet — nobody's tattoo looks good in week two.
03

The milky phase

week three to six

The surface has closed, but the new skin over the ink is still cloudy, so it looks faded and grey. This settles.

Deeper healing takes two to three months. Moisturise daily and start using sun cream once nothing is open.

If something healed short, send me a photo in daylight after week six and we will book a touch-up.

Do

  • Moisturise daily, SPF 30+ once fully closed.
  • Back to the gym, the pool and the sea from about week three.
  • Send a daylight photo at week six if anything looks patchy.

Don't

  • Don't book a touch-up before week six. It is not healed yet.
  • Don't let anyone else "fix" it in the meantime.
  • Don't sunbathe on it, ever, but especially not now.
04

The next forty years

forever

Sun is the only thing that will really ruin it. Keep it under SPF 30 whenever it is uncovered.

Moisturise the skin. Fine lines soften a little over ten years or so, which is normal.

Come back when it needs a refresh. I keep every drawing on file.

!!

When to stop reading and call someone

See a doctor

Soreness, warmth and some redness in the first three days is normal. These are not. See a doctor the same day if you have:

  • Redness, swelling or pain that is getting worse after day three, not better
  • Thick yellow or green discharge, or a smell
  • Red streaks spreading away from the tattoo
  • A fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell
  • Skin that is hot, hard or increasingly tight around the piece
  • A raised, blistering or intensely itching rash — sometimes an allergy to a pigment

Tell me afterwards so I have a record, but see a doctor first. I am a tattooist, not a clinician.

This sheet is general guidance for a healthy adult healing a normal tattoo. It is not medical advice. If you are immunosuppressed, diabetic, pregnant, on blood thinners or being treated for a skin condition, follow your doctor's instructions over mine.

??

Asked constantly

Can I go to the gym?
Light movement from day two. Nothing that soaks it in sweat or drags across it until the flaking is done, usually about two weeks. Wash it as soon as you finish.
How much ointment is too much?
If it looks shiny, it is too much. A pea-sized amount covers a forearm. Blot the rest off with kitchen paper.
Ink is coming off on my sheets. Is it ruined?
No. You lose surface ink and fluid for the first two days. It stains fabric and means nothing. Use old bedding for a week.
Can I drink?
Not for 24 hours before or after. Alcohol thins your blood, so you bleed more during the session and swell more after it.
When can I swim?
About three weeks, once nothing is flaking. Pool, lake and sea water all carry things you do not want in an open wound.
Do you charge for touch-ups?
Free within twelve months if it healed short and you followed the aftercare. Sun damage, picked scabs and changes of mind are charged at the normal rate.
