Biography

I started drawing on people at nineteen, in a kitchen, badly, with a machine I had no business owning. The apprenticeship came after — three years of scrubbing tubes and drawing the same rose four hundred times before anyone let me near skin that mattered.

That order of events taught me the thing this studio runs on: the drawing is the job. The tattoo is just the last forty minutes of it. By the time a needle is involved the decisions are already made, which is why I take so few clients and spend so long on paper.

I work mostly in blackwork, fine line and ornamental — heavy solids next to lines you have to lean in to see. I letter by hand, never from a font. And I turn work away regularly, usually because somebody wants a copy of something they saw on a phone. There are plenty of shops for that. This is not one.

The room is two chairs above a print shop on Ashland. It is quiet, it is clean, and the coffee is genuinely bad. Bring a friend if it helps. Bring headphones if it doesn't.

01

The long way round

2011 — now

2011

The kitchen years

Self-taught, which is a polite way of saying badly taught. Two of those early pieces have since been covered up by me, for free, at my insistence.

2013

Apprenticeship

Three years under a traditional artist who did not believe in compliments. Learned needle geometry, sterilisation, and how to tell somebody no without making them feel small.

2016

The Ashland room

Opened two chairs above a print shop. The halftone dots and registration marks all over this site are borrowed from downstairs — they were on every test sheet in the bin.

2021

Four clients a week

Cut the schedule in half and doubled the drawing time. The wait got longer, the work got better, and I stopped dreading Mondays.

Now

Still here

Booking a quarter ahead, guest-spotting twice a year, and teaching one apprentice at a time — properly, the way it was done for me.

In the room

Single-use needles, single-use tubes, single-use everything that touches you. Autoclave logs on the wall, spore tests posted monthly, and an inspection certificate you are welcome to read while I set up.

The chair is a proper one, the lighting is boring and correct, and there is a fan because everybody runs hot on the ribs. Water and sugar are free and I will make you take both.

If you need to stop, we stop. Nobody has ever been talked into finishing a session in this studio and nobody is going to be.
