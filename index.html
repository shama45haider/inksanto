Nothing here is repeated. Every piece is drawn for one body, once — then the drawing goes in the drawer and stays there.

The studio is two chairs above a print shop on Ashland. It smells like green soap and toner. There is no reception desk, no playlist chosen by a consultant, and no book of pre-drawn designs to point at.

What there is: a light table, forty years of reference between two shelves, and enough time set aside for your appointment that nobody is watching a clock. I take four clients a week. That is the whole model, and it is why the wait is what it is.

02

How it goes

Ask → Draw → Sit → Heal
01

The ask

Send the idea in your own words. Placement, rough size, and why it matters — that last one does more work than any reference image. I answer every enquiry myself, including the noes.

10 minutes of your evening
02

The drawing

Deposit books the date and pays for the drawing. You see it once, finished, a few days before we sit. One round of changes is normal. Three means I misheard you, and we start over at no charge.

1–3 weeks
03

The session

Eat first. Bring a jumper, headphones, and no hangover. We stencil, you approve the placement in the mirror, and then it is needles and conversation until it is done. Breaks whenever you want them.

3–7 hours, one sitting where possible
04

The healing

You leave with a wrap, a written sheet, and my number. The first two weeks decide how the piece looks for the next forty years, so this part is not optional and it is not complicated.
